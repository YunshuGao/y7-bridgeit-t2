🔨 Build Your Bridge — Paddle Pop Stick Tutorial

Take your simple build sheet and turn it into a strong, well-glued bridge — one tiny step at a time. Read EACH step out loud with your partner. Tick the box when you finish. The page remembers everything for you.

⏱️ 5 lessons total 🪵 135 paddle pop sticks 👥 Pair work 📐 NESA: TE4-1DP, TE4-3DP, TE4-5AG
Today's mission Lesson 1 — Build the Road (the long Warren-truss beam, 23 plain sticks). By the end of the lesson your road must be glued, trimmed, named, and resting on the drying shelf.
Teacher prep · 10 min before bell

Lesson 1 — Build the Road · Quick setup

  1. Pre-bag the sticks if not already done — 135 per pair (119 plain + 16 with holes). Keep the holed sticks in a separate small bag inside the main bag.
  2. Lay out per pair: 1 bag of sticks · 1 PVA glue bottle · 1 pair of scissors · 1 ruler · 1 sheet of A3 baking paper or scrap card · 1 marker pen · 1 damp paper towel.
  3. Project this page on the board, set to Lesson 1. Students follow on their own device or in pairs.
  4. Drying shelf: clear a flat shelf with name labels ready. Roads need to dry overnight — do NOT stack.
  5. Float points are at the 4 "Show Ms Gao" gates — use them to confirm correct stick count, correct pattern layout, and correct gluing technique before students proceed.
  6. Exit ticket: the reflection box at the bottom of Lesson 1 — students type one sentence and click Save.

Pacing milestones

  • 5 min — workspace set
  • 15 min — 23 + 16 sticks counted, partner-checked
  • 30 min — Warren pattern laid out (no glue), Show Ms Gao Gate 2
  • 50 min — all glue done, Show Ms Gao Gate 3
  • 60 min — trimmed, on shelf, reflection done

Common student errors to pre-empt

  • Using holed sticks for the road (wrong — they're for sides)
  • Big glue blobs that take 20 min to dry
  • Top and bottom chord not parallel (the truss goes wonky)
  • Picking up the road BEFORE glue dries → cracks

🦺 3 safety rules

  • 🧴 PVA glue is white and looks like milk. Do NOT put it in your mouth or eyes. If it gets on your skin — wipe it off with a damp paper towel.
  • ✂️ Scissors are for trimming sticks only. Always point them AWAY from your partner. Pass them handle first.
  • 🚶 Walking with wet glue? Hold the baking paper FLAT with both hands. Walk slowly. Do NOT run.

📚 Glossary — the 8 words you must know

stick
A thin flat piece of wood that looks like an ice cream stick. We have 135 of them.
plain
A stick with no holes. We have 119 plain sticks.
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
holed
A stick with 3 small holes drilled through it. We have 16 holed sticks. They are special — for sides only.
━━○━━━○━━━○━━
overlap
When the end of one stick lies on top of the end of another. Like shaking hands.
━━━━━━━━
━━━━━━━━
joint
The spot where two sticks meet. Every joint needs glue.
chord
A long flat rail made of sticks joined end-to-end. The road has a top chord and a bottom chord — like railway tracks lying flat.
diagonal
A stick that leans on a slope, connecting the top chord to the bottom chord. Like the slash key on your keyboard.
╱ ╲ ╱
vertical
A stick that stands straight up, connecting the top chord to the bottom chord. Like the letter "I".
│ │ │
truss
A pattern of triangles made of sticks. Triangles are the strongest shape in engineering — they don't squash.
Warren truss
A truss that uses a W-pattern of diagonals — like a row of triangles standing on their points and on their flat sides. This is the road we'll build today.

LESSON 1

Build the Road — Warren Truss Beam

⏱️ 60 min 🪵 23 plain sticks 👥 Pair work 🎯 Outcome TE4-3DP
By the end of this lesson, you and your partner will have ONE glued road beam made from 23 plain paddle pop sticks, in a Warren truss pattern with 5 triangular bays. Your road will be named, dated, and resting on the drying shelf at the front of the room.

Materials check — tick each one before you start

Phase A · Set up your workspace5 min
1 Clear your desk completely. 2 min

Right now your desk probably has pencil cases, water bottles, and books. We need a completely empty desk to build on.

  1. Pick up your pencil case. Put it on the floor next to your chair.
  2. Pick up your water bottle. Put it on the floor too.
  3. Pick up your books and laptop. Put them on a separate surface — the chair behind you, or a side bench.
  4. Wipe your desk with the damp paper towel — get rid of dust and crumbs.
What good looks like: a totally empty, clean desk. Like a blank canvas.
2 Lay your "build mat" — the baking paper. 1 min
  1. Take the sheet of baking paper.
  2. Lay it FLAT in the middle of your desk. Smooth it out so there are no wrinkles.
  3. The shiny side faces up. Glue does not stick to baking paper — that's why we use it.
What good looks like: one big flat sheet of baking paper covering most of the desk. The corners can be taped down with a tiny piece of tape if it curls.
Pro tip: when your bridge is dry, you can peel it off the baking paper without breaking it.
3 Arrange your tools at the TOP of the mat in a neat row. 1 min

Tools always live at the TOP of the mat — the area you're building on stays clear.

  1. Put the glue bottle at the top-left.
  2. Put the scissors next to the glue (handles facing you).
  3. Put the ruler next to the scissors.
  4. Put the marker pen next to the ruler.
  5. Put the damp paper towel at the top-right.
[GLUE] [SCISSORS] [RULER] [MARKER] [TOWEL] ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────── │ │ │ ← YOUR BUILD AREA HERE → │ │ │ │ │ ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
4 Decide who is the BUILDER and who is the CHECKER. 30 sec

You will both do every job — but you start with one role each.

  1. Builder — does the picking up, the placing, and the gluing.
  2. Checker — reads the steps OUT LOUD, holds sticks in place while glue tacks, and ticks the box.
  3. You will swap at the START of each new Phase. So you'll switch about 4 times this lesson.
Pro tip: the checker is just as important as the builder — a good checker stops mistakes BEFORE they happen.
5 Write your names on the corner of the baking paper. 1 min
  1. Pick up the marker.
  2. In the top-left corner of the baking paper, write: your first names + your class (e.g. John D. & Jane D. — 7TECJ).
  3. Write big — at least 2 cm tall letters. About as big as your thumbnail.
If the marker bleeds through the paper: use a pencil instead, or write on a sticky label and stick it to the corner.
👩‍🏫

Show Ms Gao — Gate 1: Workspace ready

Raise your hand. Wait for Ms Gao to walk by and look at your desk. She will check that your build mat is laid flat, your tools are arranged at the top, and your names are written on the corner.

Do NOT open your bag of sticks until Ms Gao ticks you off here.

Phase B · Count out your 23 road sticks (carefully!)10 min
⚠️ Counting matters! If you start gluing with the wrong number of sticks, you'll have to take it apart. Take your time here.
6 Open the bag and tip ALL the sticks gently onto the mat. 1 min
  1. Hold the bag over the centre of your build mat.
  2. Tip slowly — let the sticks slide out, don't shake. We don't want sticks rolling onto the floor.
  3. Spread the pile out gently with your hand so you can see each stick.
What good looks like: a flat-ish pile of 135 sticks spread across the middle of your mat. None on the floor.
If sticks roll onto the floor: stop. Pick them up carefully. Add them back to the pile. Don't step on them.
7 Find every stick with HOLES in it. Move them into a small pile on the LEFT. 3 min

Holed sticks have 3 small round holes drilled through them. Look at the glossary picture if you forgot what they look like.

  1. Use one finger to scan slowly over the pile.
  2. Each time you spot a holed stick, pick it up and put it on the LEFT side of your mat in a small pile.
  3. Keep going until you have looked at every stick.
  4. Now COUNT the holed pile. There should be 16. Count out loud: "one, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, nine, ten, eleven, twelve, thirteen, fourteen, fifteen, sixteen."
HOLED STICK looks like: ━━━━○━━━━○━━━━○━━━━ PLAIN STICK looks like: ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
What good looks like: exactly 16 holed sticks in a small pile on the left. No holed sticks left in the main pile.
If you can't find 16: stop and re-check. Look once more — sometimes a stick is half-buried. If you still can't find 16, raise your hand and ask Ms Gao for replacements before continuing.
8 Put the 16 holed sticks BACK INTO the bag and seal it shut. 1 min
  1. Pick up your 16 holed sticks.
  2. Put them ALL back into the empty plastic bag.
  3. Twist the top shut, or zip the seal closed.
  4. With the marker, write on the bag: "DON'T TOUCH — Lesson 3 only".
  5. Place this bag at the very back corner of your desk. Far away from where you'll be working.
Pro tip: putting the holed sticks AWAY now means there's no chance you'll accidentally use them in the road. Out of sight, out of mind.
9 Count out 23 PLAIN sticks for the road. 3 min

From the big pile of 119 plain sticks left on your mat, you need to take out exactly 23. Twenty-three. Two-three.

The easy way to count 23:

  1. Lay sticks side by side in groups of FIVE.
  2. Make FOUR groups of 5 — that's 20 sticks (5+5+5+5).
  3. Then add 3 more sticks. Now you have 20 + 3 = 23.
  4. Move these 23 sticks to the LEFT side of your mat.
Group your 23 sticks like this — easier to count: ●●●●● ●●●●● ●●●●● ●●●●● ●●● 5 5 5 5 3 ──────────────────────────────────── 5 + 5 + 5 + 5 + 3 = 23 ✓
10 Put the LEFTOVER 96 plain sticks back in the bag for next time. 2 min
  1. Scoop up all the plain sticks that are NOT in your group of 23.
  2. Put them into the second bag (or the same bag as before — your choice).
  3. With the marker, write: "96 plain — for sides + cross trusses".
  4. Place this bag at the back corner with the holed bag.
What good looks like: only the 23 road sticks are on your build mat. Everything else is bagged at the back of the desk.

Partner check — both must agree

Stop. Look at your build mat. Together with your partner, count the sticks on the mat once more. There must be exactly 23. If not, find the missing or extra ones now.

Stand up. Stretch your arms. Wiggle your fingers. Sit back down. 🌿
Phase C · Lay out the Warren truss pattern (NO glue yet)15 min
⚠️ NO GLUE in this phase. Lay everything out DRY first. Get the shape right. Then we'll glue.
Mistakes are FREE at this stage. Mistakes are EXPENSIVE after the glue dries.

SWAP ROLES NOW — checker becomes builder, builder becomes checker.

11 Lay the BOTTOM CHORD — 5 sticks in a long straight line. 3 min

The chord is the long flat rail. We'll make TWO chords — top and bottom — like railway tracks lying flat.

  1. Take 5 sticks from your group of 23.
  2. Place the 1st stick flat on the mat, pointing left-to-right.
  3. Place the 2nd stick so its LEFT end overlaps the RIGHT end of the 1st stick by about 2 cm (about 2 finger-widths).
  4. Place the 3rd stick the same way — overlap the right end of the 2nd by 2 cm.
  5. Repeat for the 4th and 5th sticks.
  6. You should now have one long line made of 5 sticks, with 4 small overlap joins. About 50 cm long total.
BOTTOM CHORD — 5 sticks, 4 overlaps: ━━━━━━━━━━━━━╪━━━━━━━━━━━━━╪━━━━━━━━━━━━━╪━━━━━━━━━━━━━╪━━━━━━━━━━━━━ 1 2 3 4 5 ←─ 4 overlap joints ─→
What good looks like: one long straight rail, ~50 cm, four small overlap joins. No gaps. No big bumps.
If your chord is wonky (curves to one side): straighten the sticks — they should all be in one straight line. Use the long edge of your ruler to test.
12 Lay the TOP CHORD — another 5 sticks parallel to the bottom. 3 min
  1. Take 5 more sticks.
  2. Lay them in a SECOND straight line, 4 cm above the bottom chord.
  3. 4 cm = roughly the width of one paddle pop stick standing on its edge. Or, use your ruler.
  4. Match the same overlap pattern as the bottom chord — 4 overlaps of 2 cm each.
  5. The two chords should look like two PARALLEL LINES — like two rails of a railway track lying flat.
TOP CHORD → ━━━━━━━━━━━━━╪━━━━━━━━━━━━━╪━━━━━━━━━━━━━╪━━━━━━━━━━━━━╪━━━━━━━━━━━━━ ↕ 4 cm gap BOT CHORD → ━━━━━━━━━━━━━╪━━━━━━━━━━━━━╪━━━━━━━━━━━━━╪━━━━━━━━━━━━━╪━━━━━━━━━━━━━
What good looks like: two long straight rails, parallel, with a 4 cm gap between them. Like the top of a flat ladder.
13 Add 5 DIAGONALS — sticks that lean to make the W-pattern. 5 min

This is the tricky bit. Go slowly. Look at your printed build sheet — the diagram shows 5 numbered triangles. We're making those triangles now.

  1. Take 5 sticks.
  2. Place the 1st diagonal: it leans up from bottom-LEFT to top-RIGHT. The bottom end touches the start of the bottom chord. The top end touches the top chord.
  3. Place the 2nd diagonal right next to it, leaning the OTHER way: down from top-LEFT to bottom-RIGHT. Together with the 1st, it forms a "V" or upside-down "V" shape.
  4. Place the 3rd diagonal leaning the same way as the 1st.
  5. Place the 4th diagonal leaning the same way as the 2nd.
  6. Place the 5th diagonal leaning the same way as the 1st.
  7. Now you should see 5 triangle shapes in a row between your two rails — the Warren truss pattern.
5 diagonals make 5 triangle bays — the Warren truss: TOP ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━ ╲ ╱╲ ╱╲ ╱╲ ╱╲ ╱ ╲ ╱ ╲ ╱ ╲ ╱ ╲ ╱ ╲ ╱ ╲╱ ╲╱ ╲╱ ╲╱ ╲╱ BOT ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━ bay 1 2 3 4 5
What good looks like: 5 clear triangles in a zig-zag fence between your two rails. The pattern matches the diagram on your build sheet.
If the triangles look messy: one diagonal might be the wrong way. Compare to the picture above. Re-arrange — no glue yet, mistakes are free.
14 Add the LAST 8 sticks as VERTICALS at every joint. 3 min

Verticals are short sticks that stand straight up between the two chords. They make the truss extra stiff.

Stick check: 23 (total) − 5 (bottom chord) − 5 (top chord) − 5 (diagonals) = 8 sticks left. ✓

  1. Take your last 8 sticks.
  2. Place 1 vertical at the very LEFT end — it stands straight up between the chords.
  3. Place 1 vertical at the very RIGHT end.
  4. Place the remaining 6 verticals at the joints WHERE the diagonals meet the chords (one between each pair of triangles). That's 6 joints.
All 23 sticks — verticals added at joints (│): TOP ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━ │ ╲ ╱╲ ╱╲ ╱╲ ╱╲ ╱ │ │ ╲ ╱ ╲ ╱ ╲ ╱ ╲ ╱ ╲ ╱ │ │ ╲╱ ╲╱ ╲╱ ╲╱ ╲╱ │ │ │ │ │ │ │ │ BOT ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━ ↑ verticals at every joint ↑ end end
If you have more or fewer than 8 sticks left: STOP. Re-count the sticks in each part. Common mistake — using 6 sticks for the chord instead of 5.

Phase D · Glue every joint, one at a time20 min

SWAP ROLES NOW.

ℹ️ The Glue Rule: a TINY DOT is enough. Big blobs make weak, slow-drying joints.
Less is more. The dot should be about the size of a grain of rice — 5 mm wide.
15 Practise the "dot, press, count to 10" technique on a SCRAP joint first. 2 min

Before you glue your real bridge, practise the technique on the corner of your mat where it doesn't matter.

  1. Take 2 spare sticks (or move 2 of your road sticks aside temporarily).
  2. Squeeze a tiny 5-mm dot of glue onto one stick.
  3. Press the second stick on top.
  4. Hold for 10 seconds. Count out loud with your partner: "one-elephant, two-elephant…" all the way to ten.
  5. Let go. The sticks should hold together.
Pro tip: "one-elephant, two-elephant" is roughly 1 second per word — better than counting fast.
16 Glue the BOTTOM CHORD's 4 overlap joints. 5 min

Don't lift the whole chord — lift only the TOP stick at each overlap.

  1. Find the LEFT-most overlap on your bottom chord.
  2. Gently lift the top stick at the overlap. Keep the bottom stick still.
  3. Squeeze a 5-mm dot of glue onto the bottom stick where the overlap was.
  4. Press the top stick back down. Hold for 10 seconds.
  5. Move to the next overlap on the right. Repeat.
  6. Repeat for all 4 overlap joints.
  7. If glue squeezes out the side, wipe with the damp paper towel.
17 Glue the TOP CHORD's 4 overlap joints. 4 min

Same technique as step 16. Tiny dot, 10 seconds, wipe excess.

  1. Lift the top stick at each overlap.
  2. 5-mm dot of glue.
  3. Press, count to 10.
  4. Repeat for all 4 overlaps.
18 Glue each DIAGONAL where it touches the chords (10 glue dots total). 6 min

Each of the 5 diagonals touches the top chord at one end and the bottom chord at the other. That's 2 glue dots per diagonal × 5 diagonals = 10 dots.

  1. Pick the 1st diagonal (left-most one).
  2. Gently lift the TOP end of the diagonal off the top chord.
  3. Put a 5-mm glue dot on the chord where the diagonal was touching.
  4. Press the diagonal back down. Hold 10 seconds.
  5. Now do the BOTTOM end of the same diagonal — same way.
  6. Move to the 2nd diagonal. Repeat.
  7. Continue for all 5 diagonals (10 glue dots total).
What good looks like: when you tap a diagonal lightly with your finger, it doesn't move. The truss stays flat.
If glue squeezes out, wipe with the paper towel — neat joints look better and grade better.
19 Glue the 8 VERTICALS at top and bottom (16 glue dots total). 5 min

Each vertical touches the top chord at the top and the bottom chord at the bottom. 2 dots per vertical × 8 verticals = 16 dots.

  1. Start with the left-end vertical.
  2. Lift its top end. Put a 5-mm glue dot. Press. Count to 10.
  3. Lift its bottom end. Glue dot. Press. Count to 10.
  4. Move to the next vertical. Same way.
  5. Continue across the truss — there should be 8 verticals in total.
Pro tip: work with your checker — checker reads the next vertical's number out loud, builder glues. Then swap. Less chance of skipping one.
🎉 You just made 34 glue joints in this phase (8 chord overlaps + 10 diagonal joints + 16 vertical joints). Take a moment to look at your road. Be proud!

20 Wait 5 minutes for the glue to start setting. 5 min (passive)

Glue takes about 5 minutes to "tack" (start holding) and 24 hours to fully dry. We'll do useful work while we wait.

Use these 5 minutes to:

  1. Cap the glue bottle tightly.
  2. Tidy your desk — wipe up any glue smudges with the damp towel.
  3. Re-check the bag of 96 plain sticks — count them in groups of 10 to confirm 96 are there.
  4. Update the bag label so it says: "96 plain — Lessons 2, 3, 4".
21 Gently test each joint with your fingertip — re-glue any loose ones. 2 min
  1. With your fingertip, lightly press DOWN on each joint.
  2. If a stick moves, the joint isn't glued well — add a tiny extra dot of glue and hold for 15 seconds.
  3. Don't push hard! Just a feather touch.
22 Trim off any sticks that hang past the end verticals. 2 min
  1. Look at the LEFT end of your road. Any sticks poking past the end vertical?
  2. If yes, use scissors to trim them flush with the vertical.
  3. Do the same at the RIGHT end.
  4. DO NOT cut diagonals or verticals. Only trim chord ends that hang out.
Scissor safety: point AWAY from your partner. Cut on a hard surface. Pass the scissors handle-first when sharing.
If you can't cut a stick because it's too thick: twist gently and the end will snap off. Or just leave it — slightly long ends are fine.

LESSON 2

Cross Trusses — 8 Strong Pairs

⏱️ 50 min 🪵 16 plain sticks 👥 Pair work 🎯 Outcome TE4-3DP
Build 8 cross trusses by gluing 16 plain sticks together in pairs. Each pair must overlap by EXACTLY 20 mm. These will tie the LEFT side of your bridge to the RIGHT side later.

Materials check

Phase A · Set up & count8 min
1Set up your workspace (same as Lesson 1).3 min
  1. Clear the desk. Wipe with damp towel.
  2. Lay baking paper flat in the middle.
  3. Tools at the top in a row: glue, scissors, ruler, pencil, damp towel.
  4. Write your names on the corner.
2Count out 16 plain sticks from your bag.3 min
  1. Open your bag.
  2. Take out exactly 16 plain sticks (no holes!). Count in groups of 5 + 5 + 5 + 1 = 16, or 4 groups of 4 = 16.
  3. Put the leftovers (80 plain + 16 holed) BACK in the bag and seal it.
What good looks like: 16 plain sticks in a small pile on your mat. Bag sealed and pushed to the back of the desk.

Partner check — confirm 16 sticks

Both partners count the pile of sticks on the mat. There must be exactly 16.

Phase B · Mark the 20 mm overlap line5 min
3Make a "20 mm jig" — a marked stick to copy from.5 min

A "jig" is an engineer's tool — a thing you use to make sure every part comes out the same. Ours is just a stick with a pencil mark.

  1. Take ONE stick from your pile of 16 — call it the JIG STICK.
  2. Put the ruler against it.
  3. Find 20 mm on the ruler (= 2 cm).
  4. With the pencil, draw a small line on the stick exactly at the 20 mm mark.
  5. This is your overlap target. Every pair must overlap exactly to this line.
Your JIG stick: ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━ ↑ ↑ end 20 mm mark
Phase C · Glue 8 pairs (the production line)28 min
ℹ️ Set up an "assembly line" with your partner. Builder places sticks and applies glue. Checker measures the overlap and counts to 10 for each hold. Swap roles after pair #4.
4Glue Pair #1 with a 20 mm overlap.3 min
  1. Take 2 plain sticks (not the jig stick).
  2. Place stick A flat on the mat.
  3. Apply a 5-mm dot of PVA glue near one end of stick A.
  4. Place stick B on top of stick A, end aligned so the overlap matches the 20 mm line on your jig stick.
  5. Press and hold for 10 seconds (one-elephant…ten-elephant).
  6. Set Pair #1 aside on the right of your mat. Don't move it for at least 2 minutes.
What good looks like: Pair #1 looks like a long stick with a fat middle. The overlap is exactly 20 mm — same as your jig.
5Glue Pairs #2–4 the same way.9 min
  1. Repeat step 4 for the next 3 pairs.
  2. Each time, check overlap with the jig.
  3. Set each finished pair aside in a row.
  4. By now you should have 4 finished pairs on your mat.
SWAP ROLES! Stretch your hands. Wiggle your shoulders. 🌿
6Glue Pairs #5–8 (final 4).12 min
  1. Same technique — 5-mm dot, 20 mm overlap, hold 10 seconds.
  2. By the end, you should have 8 finished pairs.
  3. You used 16 sticks total (8 pairs × 2 sticks). The jig stick can stay on the mat — you'll bag it for later.
Phase D · Inspect and store9 min
7Test each pair gently — they must NOT bend at the joint.5 min
  1. Hold each pair flat between your fingertips.
  2. Lightly press in the middle.
  3. The joint should stay rigid. If it flexes or cracks, add another small dot of glue and hold for 30 seconds.
